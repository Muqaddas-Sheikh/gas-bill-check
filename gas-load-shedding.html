Gas Load Shedding Schedule — SNGPL and SSGC

Gas load shedding means the gas supply is switched off for fixed hours, usually in winter when demand is higher than supply. This page explains why SNGPL and SSGC cut gas in winter, what the usual pattern looks like, how to check your area schedule, and how to cope safely. Timings differ by area and change with the season, so always confirm your local schedule with the company.

What Is Gas Load Shedding?

Load shedding is a planned cut in supply. Instead of gas flowing all day, it is supplied only during set hours, and the pressure drops or stops outside those hours. It is different from a fault: a fault is unplanned and affects a small area, while load shedding is planned and affects whole towns or regions.

Why It Happens in Winter

In winter, households burn far more gas for geysers and heaters, while the supply of gas stays roughly the same. When demand passes supply, the companies ration what is available so that every area gets some gas during the day. That is why load shedding is worst in the coldest months, December to February, and eases in summer.

Low pressure is part of the same problem. When too many homes draw gas at once, the pressure in the pipelines falls, and stoves and geysers burn weakly or go out. If your gas pressure drops every evening in winter, it is usually load shedding, not a fault in your own connection.

The Usual Winter Pattern

SNGPL and SSGC usually keep gas flowing around meal times, early morning, midday and evening, when cooking demand is highest. Outside those hours the supply may be off or very low. The exact hours differ from city to city and are announced by the company for each winter, so this page gives no fixed timetable: a timetable copied from another city or another year will be wrong for your area.

How to Check Your Area's Schedule

  1. Call helpline 1199 and ask for the load shedding hours in your area.
  2. Check the company's website, sngpl.com.pk or ssgc.com.pk, for winter notices.
  3. Ask at your nearest regional office. The SNGPL regional offices page lists addresses and phone numbers.
  4. Watch for announcements in local newspapers at the start of winter.

Coping With Load Shedding

  • Cook early: Prepare meals during supply hours and keep food warm, instead of waiting for the gas to return.
  • Store hot water: Heat water while the gas is on and keep it in an insulated container for later use.
  • Keep a small LPG cylinder as backup: For cooking only, with proper safety checks on the regulator and hose.
  • Never leave a stove burner on while the gas is off: When the supply returns, gas can fill the room before you notice.
  • Do not fit a suction pump or compressor to the gas line: Pulling extra gas this way is illegal, it starves your neighbours' supply, and it can damage the network. The penalty is explained on the compressor use penalty page.

When to Complain

If gas stays off outside the announced hours for days, or the pressure is too low to cook even during supply hours, report it on helpline 1199 with your consumer or customer number ready. If the problem continues for a full billing cycle, visit your regional office with your bill and a note of the dates and hours affected.
