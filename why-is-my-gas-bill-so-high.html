Why Is My Gas Bill So High?

Your gas bill can be high even when you did not use much gas. The reason is often not your gas use. Fixed charges, your protected or non-protected status, old arrears, a late payment, a wrong meter reading, or winter usage can all make the bill bigger. Check your bill step by step to find the real reason.

Are Fixed Charges Making Your Bill High?

You pay fixed charges every month, even if you use no gas at all. Protected consumers pay Rs 600. Non-protected consumers pay Rs 1,500 or Rs 3,000. You also pay Rs 40 meter rent and 18% GST. So a non-protected home can get a bill near Rs 1,900 with almost no gas use. Check the Fixed Charges line on your bill first.

Did You Lose Your Protected Status?

Your protected or non-protected status depends on your winter gas use, from November to February. If your average use in those four months goes above 0.9 hm³, you become non-protected for the next year. This status does not change in summer, even if your summer use is very low. Heavy geyser use in December and January is the most common reason. If your Fixed Charges line shows Rs 1,500 or Rs 3,000, this is likely the reason. Read our guide on protected vs non-protected gas consumers for the full rules.

Do You Have Old Arrears on Your Bill?

Sometimes the extra amount is not this month's gas. It is an old unpaid balance. Compare your Current Charges with the Total Amount Due. If the total is much bigger, the difference may be old arrears. SNGPL and SSGC also round the payable amount to the nearest Rs 10, and the small remainder appears as arrears on the next bill.

Did You Pay Late?

If you pay after the due date, a late payment surcharge is added. It is 10% of your current charges only, not of your old arrears. For example, if your current charges are Rs 1,289, the surcharge is about Rs 129. Paying before the due date removes this charge completely.

Is Your Meter Reading Estimated or Wrong?

If the meter reader cannot reach your meter, the company sends an estimated bill. An estimate can be higher than your real use. Check your bill: is the reading marked estimated or provisional? Does the previous reading match last month's current reading? Is the reading on the bill higher than the reading on your physical meter? If yes, take a clear photo of your meter and use it to file a complaint with SNGPL or SSGC. See our guide on how to file a sui gas complaint online.

Did You Use More Gas in Winter?

Winter bills rise fast because gas charges use slab rates. When you use more gas, you move into a higher slab and pay a higher rate for the extra units. A geyser, a room heater, or longer cooking time can push your use up quickly. More winter use can also move you into the non-protected category for the next year. Use the geyser only when needed and avoid heating rooms you do not use. See the current rates in our sui gas tariff and slab rates guide.

Could There Be a Leak or a Faulty Meter?

If your gas use jumps suddenly and your routine did not change, there may be a leak or a meter fault. To check, turn off all gas appliances, note the meter reading, and check again after one hour without using gas. If the reading moved, gas may be leaking. If you smell gas at any point, do not test anything. Shut the main valve, open the windows, leave the house, and call 1199 at once. Read our gas leakage emergency guide for the safe steps.

How Can You Check Your Bill in the Right Order?

First, check the Fixed Charges line. Second, compare Current Charges with the Total Amount Due. Third, check whether the meter reading is actual or estimated, and compare it with your physical meter. Fourth, compare this month's use with last month and the same month last year. Keep a copy of your bill and a photo of the meter before you file a complaint.
