Sui Gas Connection Application Status

You have submitted your application, and now comes the waiting. Checking your sui gas connection application status is how you find out where your case stands, whether anything is needed from you, and how close you are to getting gas at your property. This guide explains, in general terms, how tracking usually works, which details you must keep safe, what the different stages can mean, and what to do when there has been no update for a long time.

Why Tracking Your Application Matters

A new connection case passes through several stages, and it can sit at one stage for weeks without anyone telling you. If a document is missing, a survey is pending, or a payment has not been recorded, the case simply waits. Applicants who track their case catch these hold-ups early, while applicants who wait silently may only discover a problem months later.

Tracking also protects you. When you know the current stage of your case, you can answer questions precisely, follow up with the right office, and keep a clear record of every step. If a dispute arises later, that record is your evidence of what happened and when.

What You Need Before You Can Track a Case

To check the status of an application, the company first needs to identify your case. The single most important item is your application or reference number, which is given when your application is accepted. Without it, staff have to search by name and address, which is slower and less reliable.

Keep these details together in one safe place:

  • Your application or reference number, exactly as written on your receipt.
  • The date you submitted the application and the office where you submitted it.
  • Your full name and national identity card number, as recorded on the application.
  • The exact property address given in the application.
  • Copies of every document you submitted.
  • Any demand notice received, along with its payment receipt if you have paid it.
  • Your contact number, so the company can reach you if something is needed.

If you applied through an online channel, also keep your login details and any confirmation message or email you received. A screenshot or printout of the confirmation is a useful backup.

Ways to Check the Status

The exact tracking options depend on the company and the area, and they can change over time, so confirm the current options with the company itself. In general, applicants can check status through one or more of these routes:

  • Helpline. Call the gas company helpline 1199, give your reference number, and ask for the current stage of your case. This is usually the quickest option, and it also lets you ask what, if anything, is needed from you.
  • Customer service centre. Visit the local office where you applied, with your receipt and identity card. Staff can look up the case file and explain the current position in detail.
  • Online portal or app. Where the company offers an online tracking facility, you can enter your reference number to see the current stage. Availability of this option varies, so ask the company whether it exists for your area.
  • Written enquiry. For a formal record, you can submit a written enquiry at the office and keep a stamped copy. This is useful when a case has been delayed for a long time.

Whichever route you use, note the date of your enquiry, the stage you were told, and the name of the person who gave you the information. These notes turn a vague "I asked before" into a precise follow-up.

What the Stages Can Mean

Different companies and offices may use different words for the stages, but the general sequence is similar. Understanding what each stage usually means helps you judge whether your case is moving normally or stuck.

  • Application received. Your form and documents have been accepted and entered into the system. At this point, the case is waiting for document verification or for a survey to be scheduled.
  • Under verification or scrutiny. Staff are checking your documents and your claim to the property. If something is missing or unclear, the case pauses here until you provide it.
  • Survey pending or survey done. A survey visit is awaited, or has been completed. After the survey, the company works out the installation requirements and prepares the demand notice.
  • Demand notice issued. The company has calculated the charges for the connection work and issued a notice for payment. The case moves forward once you pay within the stated period.
  • Payment received or verified. Your payment has been recorded and the case is in the queue for installation work. If a payment was made but not yet recorded, the case may appear stuck at this stage.
  • Installation in progress or completed. The service line and meter work is under way or finished. After installation, safety checks are carried out before the supply is turned on.
  • Connection active. The supply is on, your consumer number has been issued, and your case is closed. Your first bill will follow in the normal billing cycle.

Some stages can look alarming but are normal. A case that shows "under verification" for a few weeks, for example, may simply be waiting its turn in a busy office. The concern arises when a stage does not change for an unusually long time and no one can explain why.

What to Do If There Is No Update

If your status has not changed for a long time, work through these steps in order:

  • Confirm the stage yourself through the helpline or office, and ask specifically what is holding the case at that stage.
  • Ask whether any document, payment, or action is outstanding from your side, and complete it promptly if so.
  • If nothing is outstanding, ask for the next expected step and a reasonable date by which it should happen.
  • Visit the office in person if phone follow-ups are not producing answers. Bring your receipt, documents, and notes from previous enquiries.
  • Submit a written enquiry and keep a stamped copy, so there is a formal record of your follow-up.
  • If the delay continues, escalate through the company's complaint system, quoting your reference number and the dates of your earlier enquiries.

Stay polite but specific in every interaction. "My application number is this, it has been at the survey stage since this date, please tell me what is needed to move it forward" gets a better response than a general complaint about delay.

After Approval: What Comes Next

Once your status shows the connection as active, note your consumer number immediately. For SNGPL connections it is an 11-digit number, and for SSGC connections it is a 10-digit number. You will need it for bill checks, payments, and complaints. Read your meter when the supply starts, keep the installation record safe, and check your first bill carefully against your meter reading.

If the first bill shows an unexpected amount, an old balance, or a reading that does not match your meter, raise it promptly with the company while the case is still fresh. Early questions are much easier to resolve than ones raised months later.
