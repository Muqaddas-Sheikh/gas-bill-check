Sui Gas Low Pressure Complaint

A sui gas low pressure complaint is often needed when gas supply becomes weak, intermittent or absent at times when you normally expect it, making cooking or heating difficult. Before you complain, a few simple checks and a clear note of the pattern — your area, the times it happens, and how long it lasts — can make your complaint much easier for the company to investigate. This guide walks through those checks, the details to record, the complaint steps, and the safety rules that must never be ignored.

What Low Pressure Looks Like

Low pressure can appear in different ways. A burner flame may be much smaller than usual, it may take far longer to cook, or supply may drop at certain hours and return later. In some cases, supply may stop altogether for a period. Noting which pattern you see is important, because a problem that affects only your home may have a different cause from one that affects a whole street or neighbourhood.

Try to describe the problem in terms of what you observe, not what you assume is causing it. Say whether the problem happens at all hours or only at busy times, whether it is getting worse, and whether it started suddenly or gradually. Those observations help the company decide what to check first.

Simple Checks Before You Complain

Before filing a complaint, make a few safe, simple checks. Confirm that your gas appliances are being used normally and that no one in the household has changed a setting. Check whether neighbours in your area are experiencing the same problem, because a shared problem points to a wider supply issue, while a problem limited to one home may need a different check.

Also confirm that your account is in order, for example that there is no outstanding issue shown on your recent bill that you have not yet addressed. Do not open, adjust, tamper with or attempt to repair the meter, regulator or pipes yourself. Those parts belong to the supply system, and interfering with them is unsafe and may make the problem worse.

What to Note: Area, Time and Duration

When you record a low-pressure problem, note four things: your area and address as shown on the bill, the date the problem started, the times of day when it is worst, and how long each episode lasts. If supply drops every evening, for example, write down the usual start and end times for several days. A pattern over a few days is far more useful than a single vague report.

Keep that note with your bill records. If you speak to the company, you can read the pattern directly from your note instead of trying to recall it. If a technician visits, the same note helps them understand what to look for and when the problem is most likely to appear.

How to File the Complaint

Once you have your notes, contact your own company. SNGPL serves Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad and Azad Jammu and Kashmir, with an 11-digit consumer number. SSGC serves Sindh and Balochistan, with a 10-digit customer number. Use the helpline, the official online complaint channel, or an office visit, and have your consumer or customer number ready.

Describe the problem factually: the area, the start date, the times, the duration, and whether neighbours are affected. Ask for a complaint or reference number, and note the date of your complaint, the channel you used, and any timeframe you are given. Save that reference with your notes, because you will need it for follow-up.

Following Up

If the problem continues, follow up using your reference number, and update your note with any new dates and times. If a visit is arranged, make sure someone is available, keep the meter area accessible, and have your bill and complaint reference ready. After a visit, note what was checked and what was said, even if the problem was not fully resolved on the day.

If the pattern changes — for example, if the problem spreads, worsens, or stops and then returns — add that to your record and inform the company. A clear, dated history is the best way to show that a problem is ongoing, and it helps the company track whether an earlier fix has worked.

Safety Notes You Must Not Ignore

Never tamper with the meter, regulator or pipes, and never attempt your own repair to restore pressure. If you smell gas, or if you suspect a leak at any stage, stop and treat it as an emergency: do not switch lights or appliances on or off, ventilate if it is safe to do so, leave the area, and contact the emergency helpline immediately. The general helpline is 1199, and you should follow official emergency guidance.

Low pressure itself is not the same as a leak, but any smell of gas, hissing sound, or damaged equipment should be treated as urgent, not as a routine complaint. When in doubt, prioritise safety, leave the area, and seek official help rather than investigating the equipment yourself.
