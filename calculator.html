Gas Bill Calculator

A gas bill calculator is a planning tool: it turns a meter reading or a number of units into a rough estimate, so you can prepare for the next bill instead of being surprised by it. The figure it gives is an estimate only, never the official amount, which you should always confirm on your printed or online bill.

Estimate Your Bill

Estimate only. Enter the units used and, if you know it, the approximate per-unit rate shown on a recent bill. If you leave the rate blank, the tool will show units only and will not guess a price. This tool uses no tariff table and does not claim any official rate.

Note: protected and non-protected consumers may be billed differently on the official bill. That difference is one reason an estimate can differ from the final figure. See the guide on protected vs non-protected consumers.

How the Estimate Works

The idea is simple. First, find how many units were used in the billing period. That is usually the difference between the current meter reading and the previous meter reading, both of which appear on the bill. Second, multiply those units by a per-unit rate. Because this page deliberately does not publish a tariff table, the rate must come from your own recent bill, where it reflects the slabs and category that applied to you at that time.

This approach keeps the estimate honest. A fixed table copied from the internet can go out of date without warning, and a table that looks official can mislead a reader into treating a guess as a promise. By asking you for the rate, the tool stays transparent about what it knows and what it does not know.

What You Need Before You Start

  • Your previous meter reading and your current meter reading, or the units already printed on a recent bill.
  • Your consumer category, if it is printed on the bill: protected or non-protected.
  • Optionally, the per-unit rate from a recent bill, for a money estimate.
  • Your company name, SNGPL or SSGC, so you can check the real bill afterwards on the correct portal.

If you do not have a recent bill, use the home gas bill checker to open the official portal and view one first. An estimate built on a guessed reading is of little use, so it is worth taking a minute to read the meter properly.

How to Read Your Meter for an Estimate

Go to the meter and note the full reading, digit by digit, from left to right. Do not round it in your head and do not skip a leading zero if the meter shows one. Subtract the previous reading, which is printed on your last bill, from the reading you have just taken. The result is the number of units used since that bill. Enter that figure in the calculator above.

If the meter is hard to reach, dirty or damaged, do not force it open or try to repair it. Note what you can see safely, and report a meter problem through the official channel described in the meter problem guide. A faulty meter can also lead to an estimated bill, which is a separate topic covered in the guide on estimated bills and wrong readings.

Protected and Non-Protected Consumers

Your bill shows a consumer category, and that category matters. In broad terms, protected consumers are those whose usage falls within a lower-usage pattern, while non-protected consumers use more. The official bill applies its own rules to each category, and those rules can change the final figure even when two households use the same number of units.

That is why the calculator asks for the category but still labels its answer an estimate. Selecting the category helps you remember that the official calculation is more detailed than a simple multiplication. For a fuller plain-language explanation, read the protected vs non-protected guide, and always check the category printed on your own bill rather than assuming it.

Why an Estimate Differs from the Official Bill

Several items sit between a simple units-times-rate figure and the final amount. The official bill may include fixed charges, taxes, adjustments from earlier periods, and slab-based pricing, where different blocks of units are charged differently. Arrears or a previous overpayment can also change the amount due without changing the current month's usage at all.

Seasonal habits matter too. Cooking, water heating and room heating all rise and fall through the year, so a winter estimate based on a summer reading, or the reverse, will mislead. The guide on high bills in summer explains some of the less obvious reasons a bill can look larger than expected, even when the household routine feels unchanged.

Using the Estimate to Plan

An estimate is most useful as a planning range, not a single exact number. Try it with last month's units, then again with a slightly higher figure, to see how sensitive the total is to extra usage. If the range is uncomfortable, look first at the biggest uses of gas in the home: long showers, heating, and cooking on a high flame for extended periods. Small changes there usually move the next reading more than changes elsewhere.

Keep a simple record: the date, the meter reading and the estimate. After two or three months, that record shows your household's pattern far better than any single calculation. If a later official bill falls well outside the pattern, the checklist in the wrong-reading guide is a good place to start before raising a complaint.

Common Calculator Mistakes

  • Entering the meter reading itself instead of the units used — always subtract the previous reading first.
  • Using a rate from a very old bill without checking whether it still applies.
  • Forgetting that fixed charges and taxes are not included in a simple estimate.
  • Treating the estimate as the amount to pay, instead of checking the official bill.
  • Estimating from a guessed reading rather than reading the meter.

Avoiding these five mistakes is enough to make the tool genuinely useful. The rest is simply keeping the record and comparing it with each official bill as it arrives.

After the Estimate, Check the Real Bill

Once you have an estimate, confirm it against the official record. Use the SNGPL bill check online page if you are in Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad or Azad Jammu and Kashmir, or the SSGC bill check online page if you are in Sindh or Balochistan. Compare the units, the category and the amount due, and keep the official bill as your proof of what was actually charged.
