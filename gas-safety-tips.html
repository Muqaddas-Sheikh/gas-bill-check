Gas Safety Tips at Home

Gas is safe when it is used properly and dangerous when it is not. This page lists simple gas safety tips for your home: how to spot a leak, what to do if you smell gas, and how to use stoves, geysers and heaters safely. If you ever smell gas or suspect a leak, call helpline 1199 at once.

If You Smell Gas

If you smell gas in your home, act at once. Do not switch any light or fan on or off, and do not light a match or a lighter: a spark can set the gas alight. Open doors and windows to let the gas out. Turn off the main gas valve at the meter if you can reach it safely. Leave the house and call 1199 from outside, or from a neighbour's phone. Do not go back inside until the company says it is safe.

Checking for a Leak

If you suspect a leak at a joint or a pipe, mix soap in water and brush it over the joint. Bubbles mean gas is escaping. Never use a flame to look for a leak. Tighten the joint only if you know how; otherwise turn the gas off and call 1199 or a qualified technician.

Stove and Pipe Safety

  • Check rubber pipes and hoses for cracks: Replace them before they split, not after a leak appears.
  • Use proper clamps on hose connections: Not wire or tape, which loosen with heat.
  • Keep the stove area clean: Oil and cloth near a flame catch fire easily.
  • Never leave a lit stove unattended for long: A pot boiling over can put the flame out while the gas keeps flowing.
  • Turn the stove and the main valve off at night: And whenever you leave the house for a long time.

Geyser and Heater Safety

Gas geysers and room heaters need fresh air. Never run a gas heater in a closed room while you sleep: it can produce carbon monoxide, a gas you cannot see or smell, which can kill. Keep a window slightly open, keep heaters away from curtains and bedding, and get the geyser serviced before winter starts.

Compressors and Suction Pumps

Fitting a suction pump or compressor to a gas line to pull more gas is illegal and dangerous. It can damage the pipeline, starve nearby homes of gas, and cause a leak or an explosion. The penalty is explained on the compressor use penalty page.

Teach Children and Keep a Routine

Show everyone in the house where the main valve is and what to do if they smell gas. Check pipes and connections once a month, and have the meter and geyser checked by a qualified technician once a year. A small routine prevents most accidents. If anything about your supply feels wrong, call 1199 with your consumer or customer number ready.
