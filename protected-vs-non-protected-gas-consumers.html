Protected vs Non Protected Gas Consumers

If you have ever compared bills with a neighbour and wondered why two homes with similar gas use end up paying different amounts, the answer may be written on the bill itself. This guide explains protected vs non protected gas consumers in plain language: what the category means, how it is decided, how it affects the fixed part of your bill, and how you can check which category your own connection falls into.

What Does "Protected" Mean?

In Pakistan, household gas consumers are broadly divided into two groups: protected and non-protected. A protected consumer is a household whose gas usage stays within a lower, everyday level. The idea behind the division is simple. Homes that use gas only for basic daily needs, such as cooking and heating water, are treated differently from homes whose usage is much higher. The protected category is meant to shield small, ordinary households from the full weight of higher charges.

A non-protected consumer, on the other hand, is a household whose usage goes beyond that everyday level. This does not mean the connection is illegal or irregular in any way. It simply means the household is using more gas than the level set for protected status, so a different charging arrangement applies to it. Both categories are normal, legitimate types of domestic connections. The difference lies only in how the bill is worked out.

It is important to understand that this category is not about the person, the house, or the area. Two identical houses on the same street can fall into different categories if their gas usage is very different. The category follows the consumption pattern of the connection, not the identity of the consumer.

How Is the Category Decided?

The category is decided on the basis of how much gas a household uses over a period of time. In general terms, the gas company looks at consumption across the colder months of the year, when gas use is naturally at its highest because of room heating and hot water. If usage across that winter period stays below a set level, the household is treated as protected. If usage crosses that level, the household is treated as non-protected.

The exact level that separates the two groups is set by the authorities and can change from time to time through official notifications. Because of this, any figure you hear from neighbours, social media, or old articles may no longer be current. If you want to know the exact threshold that applies today, confirm it from the latest official bill, notice, or announcement from your gas company rather than relying on a number quoted elsewhere.

Another point worth noting is that the category is not fixed forever. It is reviewed periodically, so a household can move from one category to the other as its usage pattern changes. A family that adds a gas heater, for example, may use more gas in winter and move into the non-protected group. A family that cuts back on usage may move back into the protected group at the next review. The bill itself is the most reliable place to see your current category.

How the Category Affects Your Bill

The main difference between the two categories shows up in the fixed charges on the bill. Every gas bill has two broad parts: the charge for the gas actually used, and fixed charges that apply regardless of usage. For protected consumers, the fixed charge is kept lower, which keeps the overall bill gentler for small users. For non-protected consumers, the fixed charge is higher.

This is why two households can use a similar amount of gas in a single month and still receive different bills. If one is protected and the other is not, the fixed-charge part of their bills will differ even when the gas-consumption part looks alike. It is also why a bill can jump even in a month when you feel you did not use much more gas: if your category changed at a review, the fixed charge on your bill changed with it.

The consumption charge itself also works differently in broad terms. Higher usage is generally charged at higher rates, so the more gas a household uses, the more each additional unit tends to cost. This is a separate matter from the category, but the two work together: heavy usage both pushes a household toward non-protected status and raises the consumption charge. Keeping usage in check therefore helps on both fronts.

How to Check Your Category on the Bill

Checking your category is straightforward once you know where to look. Take your latest printed or online bill and look at the consumer details section, usually near the top of the bill where your name, consumer number, and address appear. The category is often printed there as a word such as "Protected" or "Non-Protected", or as a short code next to the tariff or consumer type.

If you cannot find it on the bill, there are a few other ways to check:

  • Look at the bill breakdown section, where fixed charges are listed. A change in the fixed-charge amount compared with earlier bills can be a clue that your category changed.
  • Compare your current bill with a bill from a few months ago. If the fixed charges moved sharply while your usage stayed similar, your category may have been revised.
  • Call the gas company helpline 1199 and ask which category your connection currently falls under. Keep your consumer number ready before you call.
  • Visit the nearest customer service centre of your gas company with a copy of your bill and ask them to confirm the category in writing.

When you check, note the billing month as well. A category shown on an old bill may not match your current status, especially if your usage changed in the last winter season.

Key Points to Remember

  • Protected means everyday, lower household usage; non-protected means usage above that level.
  • The category mainly affects the fixed charges on your bill.
  • The category is based on usage over a period, especially the winter months, and it is reviewed from time to time.
  • Exact thresholds and rates change through official notifications, so confirm them on your latest official bill or notice.
  • Your bill, the helpline 1199, or a customer service centre can confirm your current category.
