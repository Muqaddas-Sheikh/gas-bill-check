Sui Gas Complaint Online

Filing a sui gas complaint online can be straightforward if you prepare your details first and describe the problem clearly. Many complaints are delayed not because the issue is complicated, but because the company has to ask for basic information that the consumer could have supplied at the start. This guide explains, in general terms, how to complain online, what to have ready, how to write a clear complaint, and how to follow up until the matter is resolved.

First, Identify Your Company and Account

Before you complain, confirm which company supplies your gas. SNGPL serves Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad and Azad Jammu and Kashmir, with an 11-digit consumer number. SSGC serves Sindh and Balochistan, with a 10-digit customer number. Complaining to the wrong company will waste time, so check the company name printed on your official bill first.

Have your consumer or customer number ready, along with the name on the account and the address as shown on the bill. If you are complaining on behalf of someone else, be ready to explain your relationship to the account, because the company may need to confirm that you are authorised to discuss it.

Details to Have Ready Before You Start

Gather everything that describes the problem. That usually includes a recent bill, the meter reading if it is relevant and safe to read, the date the problem started, and a note of anything you have already tried. For a billing problem, keep copies of the bills involved and the payment receipts. For a supply problem, note the area, the time of day, and how long the problem has lasted.

Photos can help, but only where they are safe and relevant, such as a clear photo of a bill or, where safe, a meter display. Do not photograph or touch equipment in a way that puts you at risk, and never open or adjust a meter or pipe yourself. A short, factual note is often more useful than a long collection of unclear images.

How to Describe the Problem Clearly

Write your complaint in plain, factual language. State what happened, when it started, and what you would like the company to check. For example, say that the bill reading does not match the meter reading, giving both figures and the dates, or that supply has been low at certain times, giving the area and the pattern you have noticed. Avoid guessing at causes or blaming a particular fault; describe what you observed.

Keep the complaint focused on one main issue at a time. If you have two separate problems, such as a billing question and a pressure problem, it may be clearer to raise them as separate points or separate complaints, so that each can be tracked properly. A focused complaint is easier for the company to assign and resolve.

Filing the Complaint Online

To file online in general, use the official complaint channel of your own company, reached from the address on your official bill or official portal, rather than from a forwarded link or an unofficial page. Follow the form step by step, entering your account details exactly as they appear on the bill, and choosing the category that best matches your problem.

Before you submit, read the whole complaint back. Check the number, the dates, and the description for typing errors, because a single wrong digit can send the complaint to the wrong account. After submission, save or note any confirmation message and any reference or complaint number you are given. That number is your key to follow-up.

Following Up on Your Complaint

Keep a simple log of your complaint: the date filed, the reference number, the channel used, and a summary of what you reported. If you are given a timeframe for a response, note it, and wait until that period has passed before following up, unless the problem worsens. When you follow up, quote the reference number first, so that the person handling your call or message can find the case quickly.

If you are contacted by the company, note the date, the name or reference of the person you spoke to, and what was promised. If a visit or inspection is arranged, confirm the date and what you need to have ready, such as access to the meter area and copies of your bills. Clear notes make every follow-up shorter and more effective.

If the Problem Is Urgent or Safety-Related

Not every complaint should wait for an online form. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, treat it as an emergency, not as a routine complaint: do not switch lights or appliances on or off, ventilate if it is safe to do so, leave the area, and contact the emergency helpline immediately. The general helpline is 1199, and you should also follow official emergency guidance. Safety complaints should never be left to an online queue.

For other urgent supply problems, such as a complete loss of supply, use the fastest official channel available to you, and keep the online complaint as a written record alongside it. Note both reference numbers, so that the online record and the urgent report can be linked if needed.

Keeping Records Until the Case Closes

Keep all complaint records until the issue is fully resolved and reflected in a later bill or a written confirmation. That includes the original complaint, every reference number, every response, and any visit notes. Once the matter is closed, file the records with the relevant month's bill, so that you can refer back to them if the same issue returns.

A calm, complete record is your strongest tool. It shows what was reported, when, and what was promised, and it helps both you and the company reach a fair resolution without repeating the same conversation.
