Sui Gas Leakage Emergency

If you think you smell gas, a sui gas leakage emergency is not a billing matter and not something to leave for the next bill or the next day. Act at once: do not switch lights or electrical appliances on or off, ventilate the area if it is safe to do so, leave the area, and contact the emergency helpline immediately. The general helpline is 1199, and you should follow official emergency guidance. This safety-first page explains those steps in order, what not to do, and how to help others stay safe while help is on its way.

Safety first: If you smell gas, do not switch lights or appliances on or off, do not light anything, ventilate if safe, leave the area, and call the emergency helpline immediately — general helpline 1199. Do not wait for the next bill.

Signs That May Point to a Gas Leak

The most important sign is the smell of gas itself. You may also notice a hissing sound near a pipe or appliance, a feeling of dizziness or nausea that improves when you move into fresh air, or plants near an outdoor pipe that appear damaged without another explanation. Any one of these signs, on its own, is enough reason to act. You do not need to prove that a leak exists before taking safety steps.

Be especially alert if the smell is strong, sudden, or spreading, or if more than one person in the home notices it. Children, older people, and anyone who is unwell may be affected sooner, so do not delay while you try to find the source yourself. Finding the source is a job for trained help, not for household investigation.

Do Not Switch Lights or Appliances

In a suspected leak, do not switch any light, fan, or electrical appliance on or off, and do not use a phone, a lighter, a match, or any flame inside the affected area. A small spark from a switch or a device can be dangerous when gas is present, and the risk is not worth taking for a light or a phone call. Leave electrical equipment exactly as it is.

Do not start a vehicle in an attached garage, do not operate a doorbell or an intercom in the affected area, and do not plug or unplug anything. If you need to make a call, do it from a safe distance, outside the affected area, after you have left. That single rule — nothing electrical, nothing lit — is the most important immediate action.

Ventilate If Safe, Then Leave the Area

If you can open doors and windows on your way out, without delaying your exit and without passing through a strong smell to do so, ventilation can help gas disperse. Do not go back into a room with a strong smell to open a window, and do not spend time searching for the source. Your priority is to get yourself and others out safely.

Leave the area calmly, taking children, older family members, and pets with you if you can do so without delay. Move to fresh air, away from the building, and do not re-enter until trained help has said it is safe. Tell neighbours nearby, in general terms, that you suspect a gas problem and have called for help, so that they can also take care.

Contact the Emergency Helpline Immediately

Once you are in a safe place, contact the emergency helpline immediately. The general helpline is 1199. Explain clearly that you suspect a gas leak, give your address, and describe what you noticed — the smell, any sound, and when it started. Stay on the line if you are asked to, and follow the instructions you are given.

If you are also in touch with your gas company through its official emergency guidance, follow that guidance as well. SNGPL serves Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad and Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and SSGC serves Sindh and Balochistan, so giving your area and account details, if you know them, can help direct help correctly. Do not let finding an account number delay the call if you do not have it to hand — the address and the nature of the emergency come first.

What Not to Do While Waiting for Help

Do not re-enter the area to collect belongings, check the meter, or try to turn off a supply yourself unless trained help has told you to do so and you can do it safely from outside the affected area. Do not tamper with meters, regulators or pipes, and do not attempt any repair. Do not light anything to test whether gas is still present.

Wait in a safe place, keep the area clear for arriving help, and be ready to explain what you noticed and when. If anyone feels unwell, dizzy or nauseous, move them to fresh air and seek medical help as advised by emergency services. Do not leave a person who feels unwell alone in the affected area.

After Help Has Attended

After trained help has attended and declared the area safe, follow their instructions before using gas appliances again. Ask what was found, what was done, and whether any follow-up check is needed, and note the date, the reference given, and the advice you received. Keep that note with your bill records.

If a repair or a further visit is required, note the arrangement and keep the reference number. If you later receive a bill that seems affected by the incident, you will have a clear, dated record to discuss with the company. Above all, remember the core lesson for any future incident: smell gas, do not switch anything, ventilate if safe, leave, and call 1199 immediately. Do not wait for the next bill.
