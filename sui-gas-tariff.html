Sui Gas Tariff 2026 — SNGPL and SSGC Slab Rates

This page lists the current domestic sui gas tariff for SNGPL and SSGC homes in Pakistan: protected and non-protected slab rates per MMBTU, fixed charges and meter rent. The rates come from OGRA's gas sale price notification dated 29 June 2025, effective 1 July 2025, which was still the newest domestic notice in October 2026, and the government kept these consumer tariffs unchanged for 2026-27. The same rates apply to SNGPL and SSGC. Rates can change, so always confirm the final amount on your official bill.

What Is a Gas Tariff?

A tariff is the price list your gas company uses to work out your bill. It sets the rate for each unit of gas you use, the fixed charge for the connection, and the meter rent. OGRA, the Oil and Gas Regulatory Authority, sets the ogra gas tariff for domestic consumers, and SNGPL and SSGC both bill from the same schedule.

Gas is measured in two units on your bill. Your meter reading is in hm³, often shown simply as units. The price, though, is charged per MMBTU, a unit of heat energy. One hm³ is about 3.5 MMBTU. You do not need to convert anything yourself to read the table below: find your monthly use in hm³, then read the rate for your category.

Protected and Non-Protected Consumers

Domestic consumers fall into two groups. A protected consumer is a home whose average gas use across the four winter months, November to February, stays below 0.9 hm³, about 90 units a month. Protected homes pay the lower slab rates. Every other home is non-protected and pays the higher rates.

Protected status is decided by winter use, not by income or by where you live. If your winter average crosses 0.9 hm³, you move to the non-protected rates, and the fixed charge rises too. Your bill shows which category you are in, so check it before comparing your bill with the table below. The difference between the two groups is explained in more detail on the protected vs non-protected consumers page.

How to Work Out Your Gas Charge

Find the units (hm³) on your bill, then work slab by slab. Take a protected home that used 0.5 hm³ in a month. About 1 hm³ equals 3.5 MMBTU, so 0.5 hm³ is about 1.75 MMBTU. The first 0.25 hm³ (about 0.88 MMBTU) is charged at Rs 200, and the next 0.25 hm³ (about 0.88 MMBTU) at Rs 250. That gives a gas charge of about Rs 395. Add the Rs 600 fixed charge and Rs 40 meter rent, and the total before tax is about Rs 1,035.

The exact figure depends on the GCV (gas calorific value) used for your bill, so treat this as an example of the method, not a quote. For a non-protected home the same method applies, but the rates are higher and the fixed charge is Rs 1,500. That is why a bill can jump sharply when winter use pushes a home over the 0.9 hm³ protected limit.

Why Your Bill Changes With the Slabs

  • Use crossed a slab boundary: More units push part of your use into a higher-priced slab.
  • Winter average crossed 0.9 hm³: The home loses protected status, and the fixed charge rises too.
  • Fixed charge rose above 1.5 hm³: Non-protected homes pay Rs 3,000 a month once use passes 1.5 hm³.
  • Reading type changed: An estimated reading followed by an actual reading can cause a jump or a drop.
  • Arrears or an adjustment was added: An earlier unpaid amount or a correction changes the current total.

When your bill jumps, check the units first, then your category. In most cases the answer is in one of those two places. If the units and the category both look right, call the helpline 1199 with your bill in hand.

SNGPL and SSGC: The Same Rates

Both companies use the same OGRA schedule, so the sngpl gas tariff and the ssgc gas tariff are the same — the slab table above applies whether your bill comes from SNGPL or SSGC. What differs is the company you contact: SNGPL for Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad and Azad Jammu and Kashmir, and SSGC for Sindh and Balochistan. Both use helpline 1199. If you need to visit an office, the SNGPL regional offices page lists addresses and phone numbers.

Tariff rates change when OGRA issues a new notification, so this page will be updated when that happens. Until then, the 1 July 2025 schedule above is the current one. Always confirm the final amount and due date on your official bill.
