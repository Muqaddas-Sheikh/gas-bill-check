Sui Gas New Connection Apply

Getting a gas connection for a new home is an exciting step, but the process can feel confusing the first time you go through it. This guide explains how to sui gas new connection apply in general terms: who can apply, what the process usually looks like, which documents are commonly involved, and what to ask and keep at each stage. Exact fees, timelines, and document requirements change from time to time and can differ by area, so always confirm the current requirements with the gas company before you apply.

Which Company Serves Your Area?

Pakistan has two gas distribution companies, and which one you apply to depends on where your property is located. SNGPL, or Sui Northern Gas Pipelines Limited, serves Punjab, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Islamabad, and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. SSGC, or Sui Southern Gas Company, serves Sindh and Balochistan. Applying to the wrong company will only delay your case, so confirming your area's company is the first step.

If you are unsure, a simple check is to ask neighbours who already have a gas connection which company's name appears on their bill. You can also call the helpline 1199 and ask which company covers your address. Once you know the company, you will deal with its local office or customer service centre for most of the process.

Is a Gas Connection Available at Your Address?

A new connection is only possible where a gas distribution network already reaches the area. In streets where gas pipelines exist, a new connection is usually a routine matter. In areas where the network has not yet reached, a connection may not be available at all, or it may depend on future network extension plans.

Before you start the formal process, it is worth asking the company whether gas supply is available at your exact address. This saves you from preparing documents and paying visits for a connection that cannot yet be given. If the network is nearby but not yet at your street, ask what the position is and whether any extension work is planned. The company office can give you the current picture for your area.

The General Application Process

While details vary, a new gas connection application generally follows a recognisable sequence of stages. Knowing these stages in advance helps you follow your case and spot delays early.

  • Application submission. You submit an application form to the company, either at a customer service centre or through an official online channel where one is available. The form records your personal details, the property address, and the type of connection you need, such as domestic household use.
  • Document checking. The company checks the documents you provide to confirm your identity and your connection to the property. If anything is missing or unclear, you will be asked to complete it before the case moves forward.
  • Site survey. In many cases, company staff visit the property to check its location, the distance from the existing gas main, and the practical route for the service line. The survey helps the company work out what installation work is needed.
  • Demand notice. After the survey, the company may issue a demand notice. This is a formal bill or notice stating the charges payable for the connection work, such as the service line and meter installation. You pay this amount within the stated period to move the case forward.
  • Installation. Once payment is confirmed, the company schedules the installation work: laying the service line to the property, fitting the meter, and connecting the internal piping where applicable.
  • Activation and first bill. After installation and safety checks, the gas supply is turned on and your connection becomes active. Your consumer number is issued, and your first bill follows in the normal billing cycle.

Each stage depends on the one before it, so a delay at any stage holds up the rest. Keeping copies of everything you submit and every notice you receive makes it much easier to follow up.

Documents Usually Involved

The exact documents required are set by the company and can change, so treat the following as a general guide rather than an official list. Applicants are commonly asked to show proof of identity, such as a national identity card, and proof of ownership or lawful possession of the property, such as ownership papers or a tenancy agreement where applicable. A copy of a neighbour's gas bill is sometimes requested to help identify the area's network and connection records.

Other items that may come up include an affidavit or undertaking in a prescribed format, photographs, and property documents such as a site plan. If you are applying as a tenant, the property owner's involvement may be needed. Because requirements differ by company and by area, the safest approach is to ask the company office for its current document checklist before you visit, and to take originals along with photocopies.

What to Ask Before You Apply

A few questions asked early can prevent most surprises later. When you visit the office or call the helpline, consider asking:

  • Is gas supply available at my exact address right now?
  • What is the current application procedure, and is there an online option?
  • Which documents are required for my type of property and applicant status?
  • What charges apply, and how and where is the demand notice paid?
  • How long does each stage normally take in my area?
  • Who do I contact if my case stalls, and what reference number should I quote?

Write down the answers, along with the name of the person you spoke to and the date. If the answer later changes, your notes help you raise the matter clearly.

What to Keep Safe

From the day you apply, build a small file for your connection case. Keep the application receipt, copies of all documents submitted, the demand notice and its payment receipt, and any letters or messages from the company. Most importantly, keep your application or reference number safe: it is the key to tracking your case and to every follow-up conversation.

Once the connection is installed, keep the meter installation record and note your consumer number. For SNGPL connections the consumer number is 11 digits, and for SSGC connections the customer number is 10 digits. You will need this number for every future bill check, complaint, and payment, so store it somewhere you can find it easily.
